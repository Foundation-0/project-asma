# Separate immune history, local activation and injury

**2026-11-05 · round 2 · public-source conceptual review.** No study data were
acquired or analysed, no biological finding was discovered, and no clinical
intervention is proposed. The contribution is a more discriminating set of
competing explanations and a precise limitation of the existing study design.
Labels follow [CLAIMS.md](../CLAIMS.md).

The previous [frontier proposal](anti-yo-frontier-2026-11-04.md) asks which
antigen, presenting cell and effector function explain the CD4 response in
Petitpré et al. This review adds three distinctions: **the antigen history that
created a cytotoxic cell need not be its local activation cue; local activation
need not require recognition of the injured cell; and a disease-associated
profile need not be identifiable separately from the source of the samples.**
These are established possibilities in immunology and study design, newly
specified here as tests for this project. They are not claims of first discovery.

## 1. A specific alternative to an exclusively onconeural CD4 repertoire

**KNOWN:** Hoeks et al. (2026) compared four age- and sex-matched female donors
per single-cell group: CMV-positive healthy, CMV-positive relapsing-remitting
MS, and CMV-negative MS. Cells were enriched for CD4+CD27− expression. CMV
seropositivity was associated with more CD28− cytotoxic subsets and higher
cytotoxic expression, whereas the GZMK+ CD28+ subset proportions did not differ
between groups. There was no CMV-negative healthy single-cell group. The study
does not establish the antigen specificity of every cell or an anti-Yo mechanism.
[Hoeks, Results/Figures 2, 4–5](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXI.0000000000200561).

**CONJECTURE:** historical viral immunity could supply part of the mature
cytotoxic CD4 population seen in anti-Yo disease, while local inflammation
selects, activates or recruits a subset. This could coexist with a true
CDR2L/CDR2 response. CMV is a concrete comparator, not an inferred cause of
anti-Yo disease and not the only relevant immune history.

The phenotype distinction is decisive. Petitpré's significant Figure 4 contrast
concerned **CSF CTL-low versus IIH**, while its Figure 5 blood CTLs were more
mature. Hoeks's CMV association is strongest in a mature **blood CD28−** layer.
It therefore cannot simply explain the CSF CTL-low result. The useful question
is whether different antigen histories account for different parts of the
compartment/maturation pattern, rather than whether all cytotoxic cells are
"viral" or "autoimmune". No marker-equivalence mapping between the studies has
been validated.

**Proposed discriminating evidence:** in a separately approved follow-up, compare
functionally mapped viral-reactive, CDR2L/CDR2-reactive, and unresolved receptors
within the same donor, compartment and maturation stratum. Retain receptors
reactive in more than one context as a separate category. Measure antigen
recognition independently of the transcriptomic label. This is a design question,
not permission to acquire or express the study's receptors.

| Possible result | Interpretation that becomes more plausible | What it still would not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mature blood clones preferentially recognize viral antigens; CSF CTL-low clones preferentially recognize naturally processed onconeural antigen | Different antigen histories contribute to the apparent compartment gradient | A migration direction, local differentiation, or neuronal killing |
| The same well-validated viral-reactive receptors are observed in different blood/CSF states | Established viral-reactive memory is associated with the compartment/state contrast; local regulation or selective recruitment/retention remain possible | A state transition, that viral recognition drives the local state, or that no self-reactivity exists |
| Naturally processed CDR2L/CDR2 recognition is reproducible across prioritized CSF clones and relevant HLA contexts | The onconeural-receptor hypothesis gains direct support | A direct attack on Purkinje cells, tumor benefit, or a safe target |
| No tested antigen activates a technically competent receptor system | The tested antigen/presenter set is insufficient for those receptors | Absence of antigen recognition, an autoimmune mechanism, or irrelevance to injury |

CMV seropositivity cannot substitute for receptor-level specificity; a sequence
database match is also not functional proof. A viral response does not exclude
cross-reactivity. Conversely, negative viral testing does not establish
autoimmunity. If a future design cannot distinguish these possibilities, stop
calling its cytotoxic-state marker disease-specific. No antiviral or
immunosuppressive treatment conclusion follows from this proposal.

## 2. An antigen-negative result need not end the injury investigation

**KNOWN:** Stopnicki et al. used superantigen-activated human CD4 cells and fetal
human astrocytes. The reported contact-associated granzyme B release caused
caspase-dependent GFAP fragmentation without MHC-II dependence; astrocytes had
sublethal functional impairment rather than apoptosis. This was an in-vitro
model, not anti-Yo disease, Purkinje cells, or naturally activated patient clones.
Only the indexed primary abstract was examined here.
[Stopnicki, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/glia.23503).

**KNOWN:** Broux et al. reported that IL-15 enhanced migration and several
cytotoxic properties of CD4+CD28− cells in MS-related experiments, and localized
IL-15 to astrocytes and infiltrating macrophages in inflammatory lesions. The
abstract does not establish an anti-Yo pathway or show that all relevant
activation is independent of TCR recognition.
[Broux, abstract](https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.1401547).

**CONJECTURE:** a two-stage explanation deserves its own falsification test:
an activation event creates or reactivates an effector, then an injured neural
support cell need not present the same antigen to be affected. Historical viral
memory and local cytokines are possible inputs to that model, not established
links in an anti-Yo causal chain. The prior frontier's general indirect-effector
hypothesis is retained; the new contribution is a concrete reason to keep
activation specificity and downstream injury as separate experimental axes.

For a proposed functional study, antigen/HLA perturbation should address
**activation**, while independently specified target-recognition, contact and
effector-pathway comparisons address **injury**. A single experiment in which
blocking HLA-II prevents all activation cannot distinguish direct target
recognition from an upstream requirement for activation. A generic suppression
of cell viability would be equally uninformative. Separate controls must verify
effector competence and target-cell health, and readouts must distinguish
sublethal support-cell dysfunction from cell death. This is conceptual design,
not a laboratory protocol or an executed experiment.

The relevant falsifiers differ:

- **Direct onconeural target-recognition model:** target-cell antigen/HLA
  dependence should remain after comparable upstream activation is established.
  Failure of that dependence weakens this particular model.
- **Indirect effector model:** injury should persist under a justified absence
  of the proposed target-recognition route, but disappear under a validated
  perturbation of the proposed downstream injury mechanism. Otherwise the
  proposed indirect mechanism is unsupported.
- **Bystander/noninjurious model:** activation can occur without reproducible
  injury in a competent, relevant system. A negative result narrows the tested
  context; it does not prove harmlessness in every tissue or disease phase.

A positive peptide response alone would not resolve these models. Nor would a
negative CDR2L/CDR2 response eliminate all CD4-mediated contributions. The useful
next endpoint is a receptor–activation–injury relationship with alternatives
distinguished, rather than a longer list of elevated proteins.

## 3. A source-confounded CSF contrast cannot certify disease specificity

**KNOWN:** Petitpré's Methods/Results assign all three Yo-PCD CSF samples to
the new study and the MS/IIH CSF controls to the earlier Schafflick dataset.
Same-study ovarian-cancer controls contribute blood only. The Methods describe
Harmony integration and source-mixing metrics.
[Petitpré, accepted PDF pp. 2–3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-026-01841-x_reference.pdf).

**KNOWN design principle, conditional application:** for a specified CSF feature or cell
state, let `D` indicate Yo-PCD and `S` the new study source. In the observed
CSF disease/control contrast, `D = S`. An additive model

```text
E[Y | D, S] = a + b D + g S
```

identifies `b + g`, not `b` and `g` separately: replacing `(b, g)` by
`(b + t, g − t)` for any admissible `t` leaves every fitted observation
unchanged. This is an elementary rank-deficiency argument, not new mathematics,
a fitted analysis, or proof that the measured difference is technical. It is
conditional on allowing an unknown source effect on the specified outcome.
More cells and a small p-value cannot supply the missing contrast.

Shared cell types, reference cells or integration algorithms can support a
correction only with additional assumptions about which biology is stable and
which source effects transfer. Good UMAP mixing or an integration score does
not independently validate those assumptions. The existing blood cancer
controls may help assess some technical effects; they do not by themselves
estimate a CSF-specific source effect. Likewise, paired blood–CSF comparisons
can remove a source effect only to the extent that its compartment interaction
is absent or independently bounded. This is more specific than the previous
frontier's generic batch-effect caution.

The general identifiability problem and the value of connected experimental
designs predate this review. Song, Chan and Wei prove conditions for their
particular single-cell mixture model; that theorem should not be imported as
a guarantee for disease effects or the present dataset.
[Song, Introduction/Results and Theorem 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16905-2).

**Design consequence (CONJECTURE):** prioritize a same-processing CSF disease
control comparison and suitable cross-processing reference material before
claiming a disease-specific CSF signature. Controls need to bridge the actual
collection, storage and library differences relevant to the endpoint; sequencing
the same material twice does not erase earlier collection differences. Technical
bridges also do not correct differences in age, therapy, immune history or
clinical ascertainment. Any new collection requires its own protocol and
authorization; no new sampling or lumbar puncture is proposed here.

**Stop rule:** if no relevant bridge or independently supported invariance
assumption is available, retain the descriptive study-associated contrast and
functional hypotheses, but do not convert it into a validated disease-specific
biomarker. The paper's biological hypothesis can still be true; this design
alone does not separate the alternatives.

## 4. Distinguish an immune response to cancer from neurological injury

**KNOWN:** a 1997 ovarian-cancer survey reported four anti-Yo-positive patients
among 181, with no paraneoplastic neurological syndrome detected during the
reported follow-up. Testing used immunofluorescence and immunoblotting. These
historical assay results cannot be equated without confirmation to modern
CDR2L-specific testing, and the small count is not a current prevalence estimate.
[Drlicek et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004150050054).

**CONJECTURE:** a modern, independently confirmed antibody-positive cancer
group without neurological disease is a valuable missing comparison. It could
help distinguish a state associated with onconeural immunization from one
associated with neural injury. Antibody-negative same-histotype cancer controls
answer a different question. Existing clinical research samples, where ethically
and legally available, could address parts of this comparison; availability
has not been established.

Conditioning on antibody positivity or diagnosed syndrome can also distort
associations among upstream causes if both affect selection. That is a possible
collider structure, not an observed bias established here. In particular, weak
or negative associations among two proposed contributors inside a selected case
series do not by themselves show that either contributor is protective. A
causal diagram and explicit recruitment model would be required before making
that claim. No new numerical result is asserted.
