01 / ReproductionActive protocol

Can a published cancer signal survive a careful recheck?

Pilot 001 examines a 64-protein signature reported to distinguish cancers that resist first platinum treatment from cancers that remain sensitive. A signature is a pattern in measurements, not an explanation of why treatment fails.

We have audited sources, recorded a score discrepancy and built a pipeline tested on invented inputs. The published model has not been reproduced on study data. Initial treatment failure stays distinct from resistance that develops later.

02 / Immune mechanismsOpen question

What does an immune cell recognise—and what does it harm?

The anti-Yo frontier builds on a September 2026 study of immune cells in a cancer-associated neurological syndrome. Detecting a protein, recognising it and injuring a cell are three different events. Cells can display small protein fragments on their surface for T cells—a kind of immune cell—to inspect. A displayed fragment does not by itself prove that a particular T cell responds.

Our reviews found relevant protein-fragment annotations and alternative explanations. They have not established the recognised fragment or the injury mechanism. An evidence graph keeps each claim attached to its actual context.

03 / Research from researchSynthetic result

Could an assumption change the answer?

Building on work coauthored by Dr Barry Rosen, we compared two ways to represent surgical removal. They agree in one treatment order, yet can predict different outcomes when the order changes.

The result suggests a concrete study: measure burden before and after surgery, together with anatomy, to distinguish the removal rules. The mathematics is reproducible; it is not evidence for choosing a treatment.

Make the connections earn their place.

03 / Research from researchSynthetic result

What treatment-sequence models assume about surgery

A follow-up to a 2021 treatment-sequence model that Barry Rosen co-authored. Original synthetic mathematics shows two plausible rules for what surgery removes. They agree on every surgery-first observation, yet predict different outcomes when chemotherapy comes first, so a good fit to one order need not settle the other. It proposes a measurement study: how residual disease depends on the disease present just before surgery. It reproduces no published model and recommends no treatment order.

02 / The instrument we are building

From a molecule
to a living system.

A molecule has a shape. A cell puts molecules to work. Tissues organise cells. Organs exchange matter and signals. Understanding a person means understanding those connections, too.

A map of questions · select a scale

01MoleculesShape, charge, binding●—●

Which atoms are where? How might two molecules interact? A still structure gives a starting geometry; it does not show every shape a molecule can adopt in a warm, moving environment.

Turn a real structure in 3D ↑
02CellsReactions, signals, decisions◉

A cell is a busy chemical system. A useful model might track a few reactions rather than every atom. The important question is whether what we leave out could change the answer.

03TissuesNeighbours, structure, transport▦

Cells behave differently depending on their neighbours and surroundings. The same molecular change can have different consequences in different tissues. Location belongs in the model.

04OrgansExposure, exchange, feedback⇄

A drug must reach a tissue, may be changed by metabolism, and is eventually removed. Linking organ models means keeping amounts, concentrations, units and timing consistent.

05A personVariation, history, environment∞

A person is more than connected organs. Age, disease, treatment history and environment can change a prediction. A future personal model would need appropriate data, measured uncertainty and prospective evaluation.

These are connected scales, not a completed end-to-end model.

A model is a precise
“what if?”

We choose a few things to track, write down how they can change, and calculate what follows. Then we look for observations that could prove the model wrong.

Virtual Human, created by Rohan Arun, gives us an open foundation for connecting mathematical models across scales. We are developing a Foundation-0 fork alongside Project Asma and preparing reusable contributions for upstream.

03 / A small experiment with a big lesson

Change the assumption.
Watch the answer change.

Imagine 100 units of material: 90 are easier to remove with a treatment, 10 are harder. Apply the same treatment and the same surgery-first removal. Now change only the rule used to describe removal in the other order.

04 / The dream · a long-term research ambition

What if we could
follow a question
through the body?

From an atom’s interaction to a cell’s response. From a tissue to an organ. From a plausible explanation to an experiment that could change someone’s life.

Imagine an executable atlas of human biology: connected models whose stated mathematical properties carry machine-checkable proofs. A proof is a chain of logical steps from stated assumptions; a computer could check those steps. Every connection would expose its assumptions, evidence and limits. Every failure would teach us where to look next.

The essential distinction

A proof tells us what follows from a model.
An experiment tells us whether that model describes life.

A complete, formally verified virtual human does not exist in this project. Even verified components do not automatically make a verified whole. The ambition is an increasingly useful, testable atlas—not a promise that all biology can be captured.
01 / Understanding

See the connections
we keep missing.

Explore how a molecular change could travel through cells, tissues and feedback loops. Find the missing link instead of hiding it behind a confident prediction.

02 / Discovery

Choose the experiment
that teaches us most.

Compare competing explanations before spending precious samples, time or funding. Ask which observation would make them disagree—and then measure it.

03 / Medicines

Follow a candidate
beyond its target.

A molecule that binds is only a beginning. Future models could connect binding with exposure, metabolism, resistance and unwanted effects to prioritise laboratory tests.

04 / Making molecules

Connect an idea
to something we can make.

Link drug design to separately checked chemistry and possible synthesis routes. Yield, impurities, stability and reliable manufacturing would still need process models and laboratory evidence.

05 / Trials

Learn more.
Ask less of people.

Stress-test trial designs, endpoints and sources of variation before a study begins. The hope is more informative research and less avoidable burden, with validation and oversight for each intended use.

06 / Individual variation

Understand why
the same is not the same.

Study why the same intervention might affect people differently. Future personal models would need permitted data, visible uncertainty and prospective evidence before informing care.

These are possibilities to work toward. Their value must be earned through experiments, independent evaluation and, for clinical use, evidence of safety and benefit. Computation would help make trials and laboratory work more informative; it cannot declare them unnecessary.

06 What we still do not know +
  • The original study workflow is incomplete.

    Missing source objects and fold-generation details still matter. The owner has sent the source request and recorded Gate 0 decisions; host preflight, aggregate-only handling and the remaining amendment work belong in the roadmap.

  • The immune mechanism remains unresolved.

    A database annotation does not establish what a disease-associated receptor recognises, or which event causes injury.

    We have no validated end-to-end human model, formal proof layer or demonstrated clinical efficacy. New datasets and prospective uses require their own permissions and evaluation.

    No gynaecologic oncologist or proteomics/biostatistics collaborator has been recruited; recruiting needs a new explicit instruction. The owner has deferred external scientific review, so any result will be labelled as not externally reviewed. The frontier study's sequencing data need institutional approval and a data-use agreement, and none have been requested.
