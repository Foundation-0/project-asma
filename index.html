project-asma · ovarian cancer research · checked in the open

Project Asma

In honour of Asma Ansari, this project works toward less suffering and loss from ovarian cancer through trustworthy, useful research.

The scope is open-ended: any rigorous, permitted contribution to ovarian cancer research, across histotypes. That includes reproductions, open-data analyses, methods, curated resources and well-documented negative results. One bounded protocol is active, a reproduction study of a published protein signature. Frontier research and ranked candidate directions run alongside it, from public sources only. The purpose and measures of success define what counts as progress.

Research only. Not clinical advice, not a diagnostic service, not clinical decision support, and not peer reviewed. No scientific result has been reproduced yet. By Ahraz Arifuddin, Aska Digital. Assisted by an array of digital intelligences.

00//The mission

open-ended since 4 November 2026

Open-ended research, bounded studies.

The aim is trustworthy evidence that helps researchers answer consequential questions about ovarian cancer and, with appropriate validation, helps patients, families and clinical teams understand their options. The project has to earn that usefulness. A working model, a high AUROC, a website or a passing software test does not establish benefit to patients.

Active track: Pilot 001

One bounded protocol: reproduce and stress-test a published 64-protein signature for primary platinum-refractory high-grade serous ovarian cancer. The wider scope leaves its endpoint, gates and tolerance unchanged. Section 01.

Frontier research

Public-source research on what an anti-Yo immune response recognises, which cells present it, and whether it causes injury. Two review rounds, an executed synthetic check and an evidence-graph prototype; no study data analysed. Section 02.

Candidate directions

Eight ranked possibilities using public, de-identified or open-licensed data, plus research handoffs and curated resources. Each direction needs its own protocol and an owner gate before any study data are used. Section 03.

What the work should serve

Understanding treatment failure, with initial failure kept separate from resistance that develops later. Explaining what a result establishes and for whom. Keeping symptoms, function and quality of life in view. These are priorities, not claims that current work delivers them.

A reproducible discrepancy or a well-supported decision to stop is a useful result. Agreement is never obtained by changing the target, cohort or tolerance after seeing the answer.

01//Pilot 001

Chowdhury et al., Cell 2023

One published result, rebuilt from scratch.

High-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC) is the most common and most lethal form of ovarian cancer. First treatment is usually platinum chemotherapy. The study behind this pilot compares two deliberately extreme groups. Refractory tumours showed persistent or growing disease on imaging after six cycles of first platinum/taxane chemotherapy. Sensitive tumours did not progress for at least two years. This is primary platinum-refractory disease, and it is not the same as cancer that responds first and resists later.

In 2023, Chowdhury and colleagues published a proteogenomic study of HGSOC. Among its results was a signature: levels of 64 proteins, measured in tumour tissue, that separated refractory from sensitive cancers in the study's own data. The headline measure was an AUC of 0.83. AUC is a ranking score: 1.0 ranks every refractory tumour above every sensitive one, and 0.5 is no better than chance. At the study's strictest operating point it caught about a third of refractory tumours (sensitivity 0.35) while almost never flagging a sensitive one (specificity 98%).

A result like that matters only if it survives outside the data it was built on. Pilot 001 takes the published panel exactly as printed and asks whether the reported numbers can be recovered from the public data, and whether the patients counted as a separate validation group really are separate. It does not build a new test, and it makes no claim about patient care.

02//Frontier research

public sources only · 4–5 November 2026

What an immune response recognises, and what it harms.

Anti-Yo antibodies are found in some people with ovarian or breast cancer and in paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration, a rare syndrome in which an immune response against the tumour is linked to damage in the cerebellum. Their target proteins, CDR2 and CDR2L, are present in ovarian tumours. A 2026 study by Petitpré and colleagues reported cytotoxic CD4 T-cell states that differed between blood and cerebrospinal fluid in a small group of patients, only three of them with paired samples. The frontier asks what those cells actually recognise, which cells present it, and whether the same response causes the neural injury. Each question needs different evidence, and the work keeps them apart.

  • Source audit and two exploration cycles

    An audit of the primary paper and its transparent peer review records the actual paired cohort, the preparation and control limits, and the restricted route to its sequencing data. Prior-art searches, competing explanations, a proposed robustness protocol and stop rules are written down, with rejected interpretations kept. No mechanism is claimed.

    On main · #39Literature · no study data

  • Compartment identifiability check

    An original calculation on invented inputs shows that different mechanisms, such as selective entry of high-state cells or conversion of cells after entry, can produce exactly the same blood-versus-fluid snapshot. It uses exact arithmetic with independent count checks, quantifies one paired-test limit and illustrates incomplete clone detection. It is a methodological check, not a disease finding or a reanalysis of the study.

    On main · #39Executed · synthetic only

  • Discovery round 2: antigen, function and harm

    A bounded audit of the public Immune Epitope Database found curated HLA class II mass-spectrometry annotations for CDR2L (two assay records) and the related protein CDR2 (seven), with a recorded replay command. These are database annotations, not patients or functional responses. They do not show a human anti-Yo T-cell receptor recognising a naturally processed, unambiguously assigned CDR2L ligand. Separate reviews distinguish CDR2L's cellular functions from its role as an antigen, and immune specificity from harm, and say what would falsify each explanation.

    On main · #42Preliminary · awaits review

  • Typed evidence interfaces and the evidence graph

    A design adapted from the typed-graph pattern of Rohan Arun's open-source Virtual Human project: each claim carries its measurement basis, context and kind of evidence, so that unsupported jumps are rejected, for example from antibody binding to T-cell specificity. An offline prototype now implements it, using an unchanged, hash-pinned copy of that project's MIT-licensed graph core with original matching rules. It holds 24 curated public evidence assertions and checks 22 scoped requests against them: 6 are supported within their actual scope, 1 meets scoped counterevidence and 15 stay unresolved. It refuses joins across separate assertions, keeps unknowns and contradictions, and separates recognition from injury. Its expectations are hand-written regression fixtures, not an independent accuracy benchmark. It identifies no ligand and infers no mechanism.

    On main · #44, #45Software result · curated evidence

The frontier is the current exploration priority. Its records are a selective review, not a systematic one, and a search that found nothing does not make an idea new. The research continuation record is the entry point, with the frontier proposal and discovery round 2.

03//Directions and resources

checked 2026-11-04

Ranked directions, handoffs and resources.

Candidate studies for a small open team, using public, de-identified or open-licensed data only. Feasibility rank is not novelty rank, and none is a commitment. Adopting one needs a protocol pull request and the owner's gate before any study data are used; controlled-access cohorts need a data-access request and an owner decision.

  1. Primary platinum-refractory HGSOC protein signature. The active track, Pilot 001.
  2. TCGA-OV open-tier genomic and clinical characterisation. Serous ovarian carcinoma only; observational aggregates.
  3. CPTAC ovarian proteogenomics, cross-resource audit. How complete the public proteomic and clinical fields are, without training a classifier.
  4. DepMap ovarian cell-line dependencies and DNA-repair context. In vitro screening biology, not patient outcome.
  5. Stroma (cancer-associated fibroblast) expression contrasts in HGSOC. Public GEO expression data.
  6. Multi-histotype ovarian carcinoma expression contrasts. Public GEO expression data across histotypes.
  7. mRNA oxidative-stress and fibrosis clustering. Public GEO expression data.
  8. Recruiting interventional trial landscape. ClinicalTrials.gov metadata only.

Molecular research handoff

Assays, target heterogeneity, CDR2L biology, adaptive resistance and data routes. It proposes an evidence schema in which a serum marker, a tumour protein, an RNA level and a pathway readout never silently stand in for one another. Proposed work, for a contributor to turn into one bounded protocol.

Broader research handoff

Treatment response over time, spatial and organoid research, care navigation and symptom research, each with concrete first tasks and stop rules. It suggests prioritising a bounded study of response over time that keeps a circulating marker, a tumour target and a resistance mechanism separate.

Barry Rosen research survey

A dated, identity-checked survey of a gynaecologic oncologist's published research. It provisionally attributes 215 bibliography entries from 2,046 screened records, with 130 still uncertain, and ranks seven research questions for this project. It also verifies one public expression-data lead whose reuse rights are unresolved. These are screening results, not a complete CV.

Source registry

Dated primary-source records with locators, takeaways, verification route and limits, from the pilot's inputs to the frontier literature. A source being available is never treated as permission to use its data.

Full entries are in the candidate directions, the molecular and broader handoffs, the research survey and the registries.

04//Still missing

recorded on main · 2026-11-05

Gaps recorded before any study-data result.

The fold helper

The authors' scripts call a helper file, balanced_folds.R, that decides how patients were split into folds. The searches so far did not find it: the authors' repository at the commit checked, its reachable history and three public forks. A later public repository from the same GitHub account defines a helper of the same kind. It is a likely stand-in, not proof: its 2023 version, shuffle details and the original label order remain unknown. The main branch declares a replacement fold rule. A rerun with it would be a new reconstruction, not a faithful rerun of the printed cross-validation.

The workspaces and processing history

The figure scripts load saved R workspaces (RData files) that the same searches did not find. Which objects they held, and in what order, decides what the printed figure actually averaged. The declared reconstruction starts from the released matrix instead and does not recover them. How that matrix was itself processed (normalisation, filtering, batch correction, imputation) is only partly documented. So even the declared reconstruction cannot claim that every processing step was fitted only inside training folds.

The corrected legends

A 2024 correction fixes the supplemental figure legends only; Figure 3D is outside its stated scope. The supplement copy pinned here predates the correction, and its legends disagree with the figure images at several points. The corrected legends have not been pinned yet, because the publisher's hosts were unreachable from the research environment.

Answers from the authors

The source request sent on 4 November asks about the missing inputs, label coding and environment. Until the authors answer, those items stay open, and nothing on this page assumes what the answers will be.

A recognised ligand and its receptor

The frontier still lacks a human anti-Yo T-cell receptor shown to recognise an identified, naturally processed CDR2L ligand on HLA class II, and an independent measure of the injury it causes. Database annotations, antibody binding or a cytotoxic expression state cannot stand in for either.

People, permissions and data

No gynaecologic oncologist or proteomics/biostatistics collaborator has been recruited; recruiting needs a new explicit instruction. External scientific review is pending. The frontier study's sequencing data need institutional approval and a data-use agreement, and none have been requested. Downloading study files for a research run, a real-source input check or any fit on study values needs the owner's dated attestation of the execution context and a new explicit instruction.

05//Rules of the workspace

how claims are kept honest

Small rules, applied every time.

Aggregates only. Audits read public files in memory and publish counts, hashes and provenance. Patient-level records, scores and linkage maps never enter the repository.

Labels before numbers. Every material public statement is labelled: proven (a written proof here), verified (a finite check with a recorded command), known (cited from a primary source) or conjecture (a hypothesis or open question). A reproduced number and a printed reference value are never presented as the same kind of fact, and "not found in our searches" never means new.

Fixed before run. Numerical checks are specified, with their tolerance, before they are executed. A disappointing result is recorded unchanged, not tuned away. Each executed result ships in its own pull request.

Owner gates. A new direction starts as a protocol pull request. Downloading study files for a research run, a real-source input check or fitting on study values needs the owner's dated attestation of the execution context and a new explicit instruction; automated checks never satisfy it.

Private context stays private. Personal correspondence can explain why this work matters, but it is not research data, a source of labels or evidence about an individual outcome. Messages, medical histories, contact details and identifiers stay out of the repository and this site.

Offline checks. python3 tools/validate.py and python3 -m unittest discover -s tests run on the Python standard library, plus git in a checkout, without network access. They check the repository's records, recompute the validation-capacity calculation from committed counts, and test the audit code, the source adapter, the evaluation library and the runtime tooling on small made-up inputs. They do not rerun any audit or the published-score check, and passing them is not a scientific reproduction. The R pipeline tests run separately, on artificial fixtures in a pinned Docker container with networking off; the synthetic validation lists the commands, and the Verify workflow repeats them on every pull request. That container has not yet been checked on an actual research host. Separate network commands, listed in the README, rerun the audits. They follow the bounded-inspection rule: in memory, aggregates only, with a record of what was read and why. Neither the R tests nor the network commands are part of the offline checks. The published-score check ran under a recorded instruction, described in its protocol.
